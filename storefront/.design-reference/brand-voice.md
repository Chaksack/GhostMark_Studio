# GhostMark Studio — Brand Voice

_Last updated: 2026-04-25. Owner: design._

A working document for everyone who writes copy for GhostMark Studio — site, email,
product, packaging, social. Pair this with the merchery.co tone reference in
`.design-reference/merchery/`.

---

## 1. Positioning (one sentence)

> **Laser, print and apparel — one studio, made to last.**

(7 words. Replaces merchery's _"Good merch, for good brands."_ Says what we do
and why it matters in the same breath. The differentiator is right there:
three crafts, one roof.)

Long form, for press / about decks:

> GhostMark Studio is a laser-engraving, DTF-print and apparel workshop —
> the only place in the editorial-merch space where all three crafts share a
> bench. We make objects for studios, agencies and brands who care that the
> mark outlasts the campaign.

## 2. Voice pillars

Four pillars. Each one is a constraint, not a slogan.

### 2.1 Quiet confidence

We don't sell. We describe. The work is good; saying so loudly makes it sound
worse.

- Say: _"One sample, posted to your door."_
- Not: _"Experience our industry-leading sampling process!"_

### 2.2 Craft over campaign

The hero of every sentence is the object, the tool or the hand — never the
brand or the moment. We name the laser, the thread, the substrate.

- Say: _"DTF on heavyweight cotton, pressed at 165°C."_
- Not: _"Premium quality apparel that elevates your brand story."_

### 2.3 Specific over abstract

If a number, a material, a city or a name will do, use it. Specifics earn
trust; adjectives spend it.

- Say: _"Engraved in 0.08mm strokes on 3mm walnut."_
- Not: _"Beautifully crafted with meticulous attention to detail."_

### 2.4 Tools as characters

The laser, the heat press, the embroidery head — they have verbs. They move,
they cool, they settle. This is the one place we let the writing breathe.

- Say: _"The laser moves with intention. Threads settle into the weave."_
- Not: _"State-of-the-art equipment ensures premium results."_

## 3. Tagline shortlist

All under 8 words. Recommended at the top.

1. **Laser, print, apparel — made to last.** _(recommended)_
2. A mark that outlasts the campaign.
3. The mark stays after the moment.
4. A ghost of the moment it was made. _(from the user's draft)_
5. Three crafts. One bench.
6. Engraved, pressed, kept.

**Why #1.** It's the only one that names the categories. Merchery's tagline
works because it pairs the product (merch) with the audience (good brands) in
four words. Ours does the same: the categories (laser, print, apparel) plus
the promise (made to last). #4 is beautiful but romantic — better as a
recurring motif on the about page than as the storefront tagline. #2 and #3
are runners-up if marketing wants something more literary; both keep the
"outlasts" idea without naming the crafts.

## 5. Hero copy — three versions for A/B

### 5.1 Factual

> **Headline:** Laser, print and apparel — one studio.
> **Sub:** Engraving on metal, wood and acrylic. DTF on cotton. Apparel
> finished by hand. For brands that want the mark to outlast the campaign.

### 5.2 Editorial _(recommended)_

> **Headline:** A mark that outlasts the campaign.
> **Sub:** Laser engraving, DTF print and apparel finishing — one studio,
> one bench. We make objects for the kind of work people keep.

### 5.3 Bold

> **Headline:** Most merch ends up in a drawer. Ours doesn't.
> **Sub:** Three crafts under one roof — laser, print, apparel — for studios
> and brands that care what the object does after the launch.

**Recommendation: 5.2.** It earns the room without raising its voice, names
all three crafts in the sub, and keeps the "outlasts" thread that runs through
the rest of the site. 5.1 is the safe fallback. 5.3 is sharper but starts on
the negative — save it for paid social, not the front door.

## 6. Don't write like this

A short do-not list. Examples drawn from the user's draft and from common
storefront drift.

1. **Sword and blade metaphors.** _"Light sharper than any blade."_ The laser
   doesn't need a knife to make a point.
2. **The word "premium".** It's the most overused word in branded merch. If
   the work is good, it shows. If it isn't, "premium" won't save it.
3. **"We believe…"** As an opener, this is filler. Skip to what we do.
4. **Repeating the metaphor.** "Presence / mark / story / ghost" four times
   in one paragraph dilutes all four. Pick one per paragraph.
5. **"State of the art" / "industry-leading" / "world-class".** These are
   stickers, not sentences.
6. **Stacked superlatives.** _"The most beautiful, most premium, most
   sustainable…"_ Say one true thing instead.
7. **Empty audience flattery.** _"For visionaries who dare to dream."_ Name
   the actual audience: studios, agencies, founders, design teams.

## 7. Page title convention

`{Page} — GhostMark Studio`

- Sentence case for the page name.
- Em-dash separator with single spaces.
- One brand mention only — no `· GhostMark Studio · GhostMark` doubling.

Examples:

- `Atelier hoodie — GhostMark Studio`
- `Studio — GhostMark Studio`
- `Made in transparency — GhostMark Studio`
- `About — GhostMark Studio`
